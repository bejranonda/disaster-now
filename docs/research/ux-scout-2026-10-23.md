# UX scout: a one-page "risk around me" for Thai phones (23 Oct 2026)

> Desk research for **disaster-now**. The owner's brief: open the page, allow GPS, see the indicators of every risk at that point, and search to see another place. It should be "modern but simple", and "everyone can get all important info for their life at this single page": "ถ้าจะให้ app ที่คนเปิดมาดูบ่อยๆ ให้เปิดมาแล้วดูว่าสถานการณ์รอบบ้านตัวเองเป็นยังไง มีความเสี่ยงอะไรไหม — เสี่ยง เช่น ร้อนจัด แผ่นดินไหว ฝุ่น น้ำ มลภาวะทุกอย่าง ภัยธรรมชาติทุกด้าน". It also shows a 12–72 h outlook and pre-warnings.
>
> This is **not a user study**. No app was installed and no real phone was used. What each app shows comes from its store listing, its own web page or a news report, and every row names which. The design in §5 is a proposal for the owner, to be tested with the script in §6.

**How it was done.** Web searches and page reads ran on 23 Oct 2026, about 19:50–20:15 UTC (24 Oct, 02:50–03:15 Bangkok), from a cloud session whose exit IP geolocates to the US (`ipinfo.io/country` → `US`). Live calls were sent with the User-Agent `disaster-now-research/0.1 (+https://github.com/bejranonda/disaster-now)`: six geocoder calls at least 1.2 s apart, one HDX metadata call and one HDX file download. The scratch files (downloads, the colour-check script) are in the session scratchpad and not in the repository.

**Status marks:**
- **page** means read on the source's own page.
- **listing** means read on the App Store listing.
- **secondary** means read in a news or third-party report.
- **[unverified]** means seen only in a search snippet, or not confirmed at all.

Source IDs such as [A3] and [R2] point to the table at the end.

## Summary: the layout and the five rules

**The page at 390 px, top to bottom (§5):**
1. **Place bar:** the place name, with "📍 ใช้ตำแหน่งตอนนี้" and "🔎 ดูที่อื่น". There is no location prompt on load.
2. **Verdict card:** one action-word level, the worst hazard with the agency's own word, one sourced action, and the update time.
3. **Six hazard tiles** in a fixed 2 × 3 grid: rain and storms, floods, PM2.5 dust, heat, earthquakes, fires and smoke. Each tile shows the agency's word, a number, a 4-dot meter and how far away or how coarse its data is.
4. **A 3-day outlook table** (วันนี้ / พรุ่งนี้ / มะรืน) with no clock times.
5. **Official notices in force or ahead**, which point to LINE ALERT and Cell Broadcast.
6. **Up to three saved places** (Yahoo!'s pattern, kept on the phone).
7. **Sources, hotlines and the trust line.**

**The five rules:**
1. **Four levels named by what to do:**
   - ยังไม่น่าห่วง (nothing notable yet), blue
   - ติดตามไว้ (keep an eye on it), yellow
   - เตรียมตัว (get ready), orange
   - ทำทันที (act now), crimson
   Each level also has a 4-dot meter, so colour is never the only signal. The lowest level is never "ปลอดภัย".
2. **The agency's own word leads every tile, and our level is never milder than that word.** Official warnings are quoted, never re-graded. Inconsistent warnings from several providers lower people's response [R6].
3. **Every number says what it covers and how old it is.** For example "ระดับจังหวัด", "สถานีห่าง 4 กม." or "14:00 น.". Old data is greyed with its age. "No data" is hatched grey and is never counted as "nothing notable".
4. **Uncertainty is shown as words plus the agency's number, with what that number counts** ("ร้อยละ 60 ของพื้นที่"). Times are ranges ("บ่ายถึงค่ำ"), never countdowns. Earthquakes are always "พยากรณ์ล่วงหน้าไม่ได้" [R2][R3][R5].
5. **Search and GPS are built for LINE's in-app browser on an old phone:**
   - Text is 18 px and sizes are in rem. Tap targets are ≥ 44 px.
   - GPS is asked only on a tap. A local list of the 7,425 subdistricts (92 KB gzip) gives instant matches. OpenStreetMap is asked only on Enter.
   - The position and the typed text never go anywhere else.

### 1b. What the comparison teaches

1. **People are brought back by places they chose and by messages that come to them, not by a map.**
   - Yahoo! keeps the current location plus three places [A1], and FEMA five [A16]. DDPM lets people choose provinces [A22], and Apple saved locations [A14].
   - JMA added push because people did not notice when the colours changed on the map [A4].
   - For a web page opened from LINE, push isn't available. Web Push isn't supported in Android WebView or iOS WKWebView (MDN data, read by the sister project; Hazard-huntz 09 §6a). LINE's LIFF browser lists Add to Home Screen and service workers as unsupported [G6].
   - **So the page's own return hooks are:**
     - a summary that is new every day;
     - saved places, including the parents' home;
     - a picture to forward to the family group;
     - Add to Home Screen, outside LINE only.
   - **Push stays with DDPM's Cell Broadcast and LINE ALERT,** and the page points to them.
2. **The apps that lead with a map serve enthusiasts.** Windy (50+ maps, 15+ models [A7]) and Zoom Earth [A8] are examples.
   - The sister app FloodWatch measured 0.5–0.7 MB per phone load and 1.6–1.9 MB on desktop, mostly map tiles (UX_VALIDATION, Round 12).
   - A map is heavy for prepaid data, and JMA's experience says a map alone isn't noticed.
   - **A map goes behind a tap, not on the first screen.**
3. **Levels named by actions are the international norm for warnings people act on.**
   - Android: Be Aware / Be Ready / Take Action [A13]. Met Office: amber "think about changing your plans and taking action", red "take action now" [A29].
   - NWS HeatRisk names who is affected at each of its five levels [A28].
   - Japan puts the level number into every warning's name since 29 May 2026 ("レベル３大雨警報") [A30], and its key line is "警戒レベル４までに必ず避難！" [A31].
4. **Honest apps say when data is old, approximate or missing.** Examples:
   - Zoom Earth's "not live!" [A8]
   - Flood Hub's "approximate … check official sources" [A11]
   - JMA's "no criteria where nobody lives" [A6]
   - Japan's separate colours for "nothing issued" (white) and "level 1" (light grey, 200,200,200) [A32]
   - FloodWatch's "ไม่ทราบ" grey for readings over 24 h old (GUIDELINES §6.10)
5. **Thai official apps rate poorly and say little in their listings.** Air4Thai has 2.2 from 311 ratings, and Thai Disaster Alert and Thai Weather have 2–4 ratings [A17][A18][A22]. None of the three listings mentions a widget.
   - **The gap disaster-now can fill is one plain page that gathers them, names each, and says what to do.** It doesn't replace any of them.
6. **In Thai floods the shared items are weather pages' warnings, TMD's clips, help numbers and humour** [A27]. The forwarded item is a picture (Hazard-huntz 07 §8).
   - **So the verdict card should be shareable as a picture** that says the place, the date, the level, the action and the source on its face.

## 2. Evidence on risk communication

### 2a. Levels and colours

- **Japan's five alert levels.** The Cabinet Office's guideline (revised March 2026, the page's own date) gives one action per level. Level 5 緊急安全確保 is "近隣の建物の上階へ移動", and the key message is "警戒レベル４までに必ず避難！" [A31].
  - **The colours were tested with people of different colour vision.** The 29 May 2020 notice [A32] fixed the colours after a test with people of normal, type-1 and type-2 colour vision and low vision. The test was run by the NPO Color Universal Design Organisation and Prof. Kei Ito.
  - Level 5 is black (12, 0, 12). It is "slightly tinted, because pure black could suggest death".
  - Level 4 is purple (170, 0, 170).
  - Level 3 is red (255, 40, 0), "shifted toward orange so it is easier to tell from black and purple". Type-1 viewers see a deep red as dark and dull.
  - Level 2 is yellow (242, 231, 0), darker so it stands out from white.
  - "Nothing issued" is white, and level 1 is light grey (200, 200, 200).
  - Since 29 May 2026 the level number is part of each warning's name [A30].
- **NWS HeatRisk** has 0 Green "Little to None", 1 Yellow Minor, 2 Orange Moderate, 3 Red Major and 4 Magenta Extreme. Each level names who is affected, for example "Affects anyone without cooling/hydration". It covers 7 days and is "supplementary to official NWS heat watches, warnings, and advisories" [A28].
  - The lowest level says "little to no risk", not "safe".
- **The Met Office (UK)** colours a warning by "a combination of both the impact the weather may have and the likelihood of those impacts occurring". Each warning carries its matrix [A29].
  - This is the WMO's impact-based approach (WMO-No. 1150, 2015; Part II approved Feb 2021 [R8]). The exact wording of WMO's slogan "from what the weather will be to what the weather will do" was not confirmed **[unverified]**.
- **A counter-example: colour by hazard type.** NWS's hazard-map table (29 Aug 2024) gives 110 kinds of event 79 different colours, for example "Flood Warning" in Lime and "Flash Flood Warning" in Darkred [A33].
  - No reader can learn that. **On one page for many hazards, colour should mean level only,** and the hazard is told by its icon and name.
- **Colour vision in Thailand.** Small studies found red-green deficiency in 7.7% of 91 boys aged 8–13 in Pathum Thani (2012) and 5.17% of men in a northern Thai rice-growing population [R10]. These are small samples **[abstracts or snippets only]**. The guidance is the same either way: never colour alone, never red against green.
- **Older Thai LINE users prefer bright, warm colours and avoid black and "inauspicious" colours** (UTCC study, Hazard-huntz 07 §2.3 [S12]).
  - Japan also tinted its black to avoid suggesting death [A32]. **So disaster-now doesn't use black for its top level.**

### 2b. Probabilities and uncertainty

- **People can use probabilities if they are presented well.** The living systematic review by Ripberger et al. (2022) found 327 studies [R1][R1b]. Its two "key points" are that "average people can make sense of and use probability information if consideration is given to information presentation", and that "probability information generally improves decision quality" [R1].
- **Say what the number counts.** Pedestrians in five cities read "30% chance of rain" as 30% of the time, 30% of the area, or 30% of days like this one. Only New Yorkers mostly gave the forecaster's meaning [R2].
  - TMD's forecasts give rain as **"ร้อยละ 60 ของพื้นที่"** (percent of the area), and news headlines shorten it to "ฝนฟ้าคะนอง 60%" [R9].
  - **Keep "ของพื้นที่" every time.**
- **Words alone drift toward 50%; words plus numbers don't.** In 25 samples in 24 countries and 17 languages, people read IPCC's verbal terms closer to 50% than intended. Adding the numeric range improved agreement, most for the extreme terms. The authors recommend "both words and numbers" [R3].
- **People already expect forecasts to be uncertain, and many prefer to be told.** A US national survey found most people inferred a range around a single-value forecast. A significant majority liked forecasts that express uncertainty [R4].
- **False alarms, and what helps.** In an experiment, "very high and very low false alarm rates led to inferior decision making". Lowering the rate slightly did not matter, but "adding a probabilistic uncertainty estimate in the forecasts improved both compliance and decision quality" [R5].
  - So an honest range helps more than hiding uncertainty to look sure.
- **The sister app's rules agree** (FloodWatch GUIDELINES §6):
  - ranges and a confidence level, never minute countdowns;
  - "ยังประเมินไม่ได้" when new heavy rain makes a recovery date a guess;
  - confidence as counts from its own record ("6 ใน 10", ≥ 30 cases), never a model band as a percent;
  - no "~" in text (it reads as a minus); "ประมาณ" or "ราว" instead;
  - "อีก N ชม." for the future and "N ชม. ที่ผ่านมา" for the past.

### 2c. "What to do" next to each level

- **Impact plus advice works better.** In a Swiss survey experiment, people given impact-based warnings, with or without behavioural recommendations, were more likely to protect themselves than people given standard warnings. Messages with impacts and recommendations were also rated "clearer, easier to understand, and more credible" (Weyrich 2020, contribution III; Weyrich et al. 2018 [R6]).
- **Conflicting warnings make people act less.** Warnings duplicated by "different public and private providers" with visual or textual inconsistencies "were perceived of lower quality and negatively impacted warning response; … the higher the level of inconsistency the poorer was the behavioural response" (Weyrich 2020, contribution II [R6]).
  - **This is the strongest reason for disaster-now to quote the official word and never invent a competing grade.**
- **What a short warning contains.** The guidance for short alerts (Sutton & Kuligowski 2019) lists hazard or consequence, location, protective action, time and source [R7, secondary].
  - The verdict card carries all five: the hazard, "ต.คลองจั่น", one action, "ถึงพรุ่งนี้" and "กรมอุตุฯ".
- **Thai evidence.** In Mae Sai in 2024, a municipal officer said residents stayed because "the warnings were unclear", that TMD's technical language lacked simplicity, and he asked for a colour code (Hazard-huntz 07 §3.1 [S24]).

## 3. Rules from the Thai audience that apply to this app

From Hazard-huntz `docs/07-audience.md` and `docs/GUIDELINES.md`, and from FloodWatch `docs/UX_VALIDATION.md` and `docs/GUIDELINES.md` §6. The rule numbers are theirs.

**Who reads, and on what:**
1. **The reader is often an older relative inside LINE's browser on an old Android phone.**
   - About 74% of Thai mobile web traffic is Android, and about 12% of Android traffic runs Android 11 or older. 71% of AIS subscribers are prepaid [07 §4].
   - 37% of people aged 60+ are offline, so a relative's screen or a picture is the channel [07 §2.2].
   - **Check at 320, 360 and 390 px inside LINE and Facebook,** as well as Chrome and Safari (FW §6.8; HH §6).
2. **Pictures travel, links don't.** Older users dislike links and avoid frightening or sad content [07 §2.3].
   - **Make the verdict a forwardable picture** with the place, date, level, action and source on its face.
   - **The tone is calm,** with no sirens, flashing, "ด่วน" or "good news" (FW 6c-1; HH §1.2, §1.4).
3. **Trust has to be shown.** Scams target the same age group, and a `.bot` address can look like one [07 §2.5].
   - **Keep a one-line trust strip:** "ฟรี · ไม่ใช่เว็บไซต์ของรัฐ · ไม่ใช่ระบบเตือนภัย · ไม่เก็บข้อมูล · ที่มา: …".
   - **Never use agency logos or LINE ALERT's styling** [07 §8.11].

**Reading and tapping:**
4. **Text is large and follows the phone's setting.**
   - 18 pt Thai was read fastest by older Thai readers in a small test (n = 18), and a looped face was understood better [07 §2.4].
   - Body text is 18 px and anything a reader must read is ≥ 14 px. Sizes are in `rem`, with a "ก+" toggle for in-app browsers that ignore the system size [07 §8.3].
5. **Tap targets are ≥ 44 px,** including inline links and expanders, and in every media query (HH rules 1, 2, 54).
6. **Colour is never the only signal, and red never sits against green** (HH §1.4, §4).
   - No flashing, and `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected.
   - Every control works by keyboard and screen reader.

**Thai text:**
7. **Thai words that break go in a keep-together list** (`<span class="nw">`). Never insert invisible characters into Thai text.
   - Style by class or direct child, never "any span inside" (HH rules 68, 72, 88).
   - Look at every new Thai label at 320 and 360 px.
8. **Emoji are Unicode 11 or older,** because newer ones are empty boxes on old phones (HH rule 64). Where no fitting emoji exists, use hand-made SVG (§5d).
9. **Plain words, one question at a time, the answer as a word first, the source once** (HH rule 82).
   - Explain an acronym once (ปภ. = กรมป้องกันและบรรเทาสาธารณภัย).
   - Dates are short Buddhist-era ("24 ต.ค. 69"), and times are "14:00 น." (HH §3).

**Numbers and data:**
10. **Every number traces to a source; nothing is invented** (HH §1.1, §2.2; FW evidence rule).
    - When two agencies differ, one is the main text and the other is named on its line (HH rule 81).
    - **Advice for the reader's own at-risk group is always shown,** never behind a tick (HH rule 86).
    - **AI never writes safety facts** (FW D-022). The verdict is built from templates and sourced lines.
11. **Each phone number says whom it reaches** (HH rule 85): 1784 ปภ., 1669 เจ็บป่วยฉุกเฉิน, 199 ดับเพลิง, 1182 กรมอุตุฯ.
12. **Show what the number measures, where and when** (FW §6):
    - Lead flood status with **cm to the bank** ("ต่ำกว่าตลิ่ง 35 ซม.").
    - Each gauge is judged by its own agency's thresholds. Never mix agencies' levels (BMA vs HII differ 0.3–0.6 m).
    - **"Nearest gauge" isn't "your home",** so say so wherever a distance-based result appears.
    - A reading older than 24 h is "unknown". Old values are greyed with their age.
    - Rain amounts carry TMD's word (ฝนเล็กน้อย / ปานกลาง / หนัก / หนักมาก). Unknown is "ไม่มีข้อมูล", never 0.
    - A point shows **categories, never a level at the pin** (FW D-021).
13. **The low state isn't "safe".** FloodWatch renamed canal "normal" to "ยังรับน้ำได้" (blue) after green "ปกติ" read as "all fine here" while the streets flooded (FW D-036).
    - **"No record" is never "safe"** (HH rule 98).

**Location and privacy:**
14. **Location is asked only on a tap, never on load.** A choice made by hand beats an automatic one (FW D-065).
    - **The position stays on the phone.** The phone downloads lists and picks what is near it.
    - **A chosen subdistrict code stays in `localStorage`** and never goes into an event, URL or request (HH rules 56, 98).
    - **Search text is never logged or stored** (FW D-032).
15. **Back closes what is open and never drops the visitor into LINE.** Sheets keep a sticky ✕ (HH rules 67, 77).
16. **Be a companion to the official channels, never a look-alike.** Point to DDPM's Cell Broadcast and LINE ALERT for warnings. **The page never presents itself as a warning system** (HH rule 98; 07 §8.11).
17. **The page works without the network after the first visit where it can, and shows old data's age.** Service workers don't run in iOS in-app browsers or LIFF (HH KNOWLEDGE §11), so the last snapshot should also be kept in `localStorage` and shown with its time.

## 4. FloodWatch's address search, and how to do it on a static page

### 4a. What FloodWatch does

Read in `web/app.js` (`renderList` 477–510, `placeSearch` 514–532, `checkPoint` 1531–1564, `locate` 1611–1623, the input handlers 1682–1686), `src/floodwatch/geocode.py` and DECISIONS D-032 and D-065.

1. **Instant local matches.** Every keystroke filters the loaded gauge list by name, code, district, province and river, after `norm()` lowercases and removes spaces. A search looks in every region. Nothing is sent per keystroke.
2. **An explicit place search.** Once 2 or more characters are typed, the list's first row is a button: **"🔎 ค้นหาสถานที่ “…” (ซอย ถนน ย่าน)"**. Tapping it or pressing Enter calls the server's `/api/geocode`. **No autocomplete goes to Nominatim.**
3. **The server asks Nominatim** (`geocode.py`):
   - Parameters: `format=jsonv2`, `limit=5`, `countrycodes=th`, `accept-language=th`, and a Bangkok `viewbox` with `bounded=0`, which ranks Bangkok first without limiting the results.
   - **Pace:** at most one call per 1.1 s across all workers (a Postgres advisory lock), and 30 searches an hour per visitor (D-032).
   - **Caching:** 500 entries for 7 days, in memory.
   - **It identifies itself** with the app's User-Agent.
4. **Thai address habits are expanded before the call.**
   - "ซ." → "ซอย", "ถ." → "ถนน", "พหล" → "พหลโยธิน".
   - A name ending in a number without "ซอย" is also tried as "ซอย…", because "ลาดพร้าว 71" is "ซอยลาดพร้าว 71" in OSM.
   - Exact name matches rank first, and duplicates within about 100 m are dropped.
5. **A result opens the point check** (`checkPoint`) and says "ตำแหน่งจากชื่อสถานที่ (OpenStreetMap) อาจคลาดเคลื่อนได้". It shows "© ผู้ร่วมสร้าง OpenStreetMap".
   - Failures say what to try next: "ไม่พบสถานที่นี้ ลองชื่อซอย ถนน อำเภอ หรือแตะบนแผนที่แทน".
6. **GPS only on the 📍 tap** (`locate`): `enableHighAccuracy: false`, a 10 s timeout, and clear fallbacks ("ไม่ได้รับอนุญาตให้ใช้ตำแหน่ง แตะบนแผนที่เพื่อเลือกจุดแทนได้").
   - A later visit with permission already granted uses it silently, unless a region was chosen by hand (D-065).
7. **Privacy:**
   - Queries are never logged or stored (D-032). Access logs strip the query string of `/api/geocode`, `/api/point` and `/api/reverse` (FW §6.23).
   - Reverse lookups round the position to about 1 km before the call.
   - **One leak to avoid:** a point link is `#p=lat,lon` (about 10 m precision). The fragment never reaches a server, but whoever receives the link sees the exact spot.

### 4b. What the geocoders allow from a browser (read 23 Oct 2026)

**Nominatim (OSMF) usage policy** [G1]:
- **Rate:** "an absolute maximum of 1 request per second". The limits "apply per website/application" and "the sum of traffic by all your users should not exceed the limits".
  - User-triggered searches are fine while the number of users is "moderate".
- **Autocomplete is not allowed:** you "must not implement such a service on the client side using the API".
- **Identify the app** with a valid Referer or User-Agent. A browser `fetch` can't set the User-Agent but sends the page origin as Referer by default.
- **Attribution** must be shown, and the data is under ODbL.
- **Switchable:** the app must be able to "switch the service at our request at any time", "without requiring a software update".
- **Proxy:** "If at all possible, set up a proxy and also enable caching of requests."

**Photon (komoot's public server)** [G2]:
- "You can use the API for your project, but please be fair - extensive usage will be throttled". There is no guarantee of availability. Its page offers "search as you type".
- **Live on 23 Oct 2026:** `lang=th` is refused ("Language is not supported. Supported are: default, de, en, fr"). The default returns OSM's local names, which are Thai in Thailand.

**OSMF privacy policy** [G4]: OSMF services collect the IP address, browser, referring page and times. Nominatim queries are analysed "for missing addresses and postcodes". No retention period is given for these logs.

Recommended order:

1. **A local subdistrict list for instant matches, and for GPS.**

   - **Contents:** its `admin3` sheet has **7,425 subdistricts**, with Thai and English names, district (928), province (77), DOPA code (TH100101) and centre lat/lon.
   - **Measured size:** a compact JSON (provinces and districts as index tables; per subdistrict the Thai name, district index and centre at 3 decimals, about 110 m) is **367,652 bytes raw and 91,818 bytes gzip**. With the 6-digit code it is 434,477 raw and 111,280 gzip. English names for `/en/` are a separate file of about 47 KB gzip.
   - **Load it when the search box is focused or 📍 is tapped,** not on first paint.
   - **Matching:**
     - Normalise as FloodWatch does: lower-case and remove spaces.
     - Strip ต./ตำบล/แขวง, อ./อำเภอ/เขต and จ./จังหวัด.
     - Map "กทม." / "กรุงเทพฯ" to กรุงเทพมหานคร.
     - Keep a reviewed list of common short names (โคราช, อยุธยา, แปดริ้ว …), compiled with a native reader **[to compile]**.
     - Show up to 6 rows as "ตำบล · อำเภอ · จังหวัด". **Only 5,838 of the 7,425 names are unique,** so the district and province must always show.
     - Match subdistrict, district and province names, so "บางกะปิ" offers the district too.
2. **"🔎 ค้นหาสถานที่ “…” (ซอย ถนน ร้าน)" only on Enter or a tap,** for street-level places:
   - Use **Photon first.** It has CORS, Thai names, fair use and no hard number. Send FloodWatch's variants, at most 2 calls ≥ 1.1 s apart.
   - **Nominatim from the browser is a fallback only while traffic is small.** Its 1 request/s is for the whole app, and a page shared in a flood could pass it in minutes.
   - **Better: FloodWatch's own `/api/geocode`,** once it sends CORS for this origin. It already throttles to Nominatim's limit, caches, and keeps queries out of its logs (D-032).
   - **Put the geocoder choice in a small runtime JSON** (`photon` | `floodwatch` | `nominatim` | `off`), so the OSMF or komoot can be obeyed at once [G1].
   - **Never call either geocoder while the person is typing.**
   - **Cache answers in memory for the visit.**
3. **Say what the search sends, on the button itself:** "ค้นด้วย OpenStreetMap: คำที่พิมพ์จะส่งไปค้นที่นั่น".
   - This is the one place where typed text leaves the phone. It goes to OSMF or komoot, not to us, and their privacy terms apply [G4].
   - Show "© ผู้ร่วมสร้าง OpenStreetMap" under the results.
4. **Turn a result or a GPS fix into a subdistrict on the phone.**
   - Use the nearest subdistrict centre, then ask "อยู่ใน ต.คลองจั่น อ.บางกะปิ ใช่ไหม?" with [ใช่] and [เลือกเอง], as the sister project proposed (Hazard-huntz 08 §2b).
   - How often the nearest centre is the wrong subdistrict near a border is **not measured yet**. That can be measured offline from the boundary file (437 MB GeoJSON) before launch.
   - The hazard data is then read from files for the whole country or the province, never by asking a server about a point (HH rule 98).
5. **Settings the static host must get right:**
   - `Permissions-Policy` must allow `geolocation=(self)`. The sister app sends `geolocation=()`, which blocks it (`Hazard-huntz/public/_headers`). Don't copy that header.
   - Keep the default `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, so the geocoders can see which site calls them.
   - GPS needs HTTPS.
6. **Share a place by its code, never by coordinates:** `…/#t=100101`, not `#p=13.75,100.49`. A family group then sees "ต.พระบรมมหาราชวัง" and not the house.
7. **GPS inside LINE is unconfirmed.** LINE's LIFF page doesn't mention geolocation at all [G6]. A third-party talk says iOS stops showing the prompt after two refusals **[unverified]**.
   - **Search must always work without GPS.**
   - Offer "เปิดใน Chrome/Safari" (`?openExternalBrowser=1`) when the location call fails inside LINE.
   - Test both on real phones (§6, task 3).

### 5a. The four levels, plus "no data" and "old"

**Our levels frame every tile, but the tile's main word is always the agency's own.** The levels' names are actions, as Android's and the Met Office's are [A13][A29]. Colour is never alone: each level has a word and a 4-dot meter that counts up, so it reads the same in greyscale.

**The mapping rule.** Use the agency's word, never a milder level than that word (HH rule 81). The word stays on the tile ("ส้ม · เริ่มมีผลต่อสุขภาพ (คพ.)"). Mapping by meaning keeps our frame consistent with what the agency says, which is what [R6] asks for.

**For the owner and a health reviewer to decide:**
- **The heat mapping.** The heat index's เฝ้าระวัง band (27.0–32.9 °C, the sister app's sourced bands) will be crossed on many days of the hot season. Mapping it to level 2 makes the heat tile yellow then. Mapping it to level 1 would put "ยังไม่น่าห่วง" next to the agency's word "เฝ้าระวัง". The recommendation is level 2, with the mild action the source gives ("ดื่มน้ำสะอาดบ่อยๆ").
  - Measure how many days the page shows ≥ level 2 in the first month.
  - A very high alarm rate harms decisions [R5].
- **TMD's CAP feed.** Which severity and urgency values it sets was **not read yet**. The level 3/4 rule for official warnings must be checked against real CAP messages.
- **Fires and smoke have no agency level.** NASA FIRMS hotspot counts are information, so that tile shows a count with no level colour. Its effect reaches the level through PCD's dust reading.

**The colours were checked by running code** (Machado 2009 simulation; CIEDE2000; WCAG 2 contrast; script in the session scratchpad):
- Label contrast on its fill: 5.2:1 (level 1), 11.2:1 (2), 5.6:1 (3), 6.9:1 (4) and 6.9:1 (no data).
- The smallest difference between any two fills:
  - normal vision 24.5 (level 1 vs no-data grey);
  - protan 22.9;
  - deutan 20.2 (yellow vs orange);
  - tritan 18.4 (orange vs crimson).
- A lighter orange (`#F28C28`) fell to 14.1 for deutans, so the darker `#E46C0A` was chosen. It is also FloodWatch's warning orange.
- **Grey "no data" is told apart by its hatch and dashes,** not by colour.
- The palette has no black, which older Thai readers avoid [07 §2.3] and which Japan tinted [A32], and no green next to red.

**Hazard tiles** (six, fixed order). Each tile contains:
- **Icon and name** (18 px bold).
- **Meter and level word** (20 px bold).
- **The agency's word and number** (18 px).
- **Scope and time** (15 px grey): "ระดับจังหวัด", "สถานีห่าง {4} กม.", "ตำบลนี้", "พยากรณ์วันนี้", "{14:00} น.".
- **Paint grows with the level:**
  - levels 1–2: a white tile with a filled badge;
  - level 3: plus a 2 px orange border;
  - level 4: plus a light crimson tint (`#FBE9EC`).
  - No flashing and no motion.
- **A tap opens the hazard's sheet** (§5d).

**Hazard sheet** (opened from a tile; Back and a sticky ✕ close it; HH rules 67, 77):
1. The level word with the meter, then the agency's word and number with unit and time ("PM2.5 52 มคก./ลบ.ม. · สีส้ม · 14:00 น.").
2. **What it covers:**
   - "สถานีวัดฝุ่นใกล้สุด: {ชื่อ} ห่าง 4 กม. ไม่ใช่ค่าที่บ้านคุณ" (FW §6.11);
   - or "ประกาศระดับจังหวัด";
   - or "แบบจำลอง ไม่ใช่ค่าที่วัดได้" (FW D-044 tiers: วัดจริง · ประมาณจากแบบจำลอง · จากดาวเทียม).
3. **ทำอะไรตอนนี้:** the sourced lines for everyone, then the lines for at-risk groups, always shown under "เด็กเล็ก ผู้สูงอายุ คนท้อง คนมีโรคประจำตัว" (HH rule 86). Then a link to the sister app's guide or tool (`/air`, `/hot`, `/checklist/<id>`).
4. **3 วันข้างหน้า** for this hazard, with ranges and the agency's numbers.
5. **Sources,** once, at the bottom, with times. Plus the ⚠️ note where a line rests on foreign guidance (HH rule 82).

**The outlook table**
- **Columns:** "วันนี้ ศ.23", "พรุ่งนี้ ส.24", "มะรืน อา.25". Rows only for hazards that have a forecast.
- **Cells give the agency's words and numbers:**
  - "หนัก · 60% ของพื้นที่";
  - "ราว 35–37 °C" (a range from the forecast, never a single exact value);
  - parts of the day ("บ่ายถึงค่ำ"), never clock times;
  - "ยังบอกไม่ได้" where no tested forecast exists (FW §6.22: no direction without evidence);
  - "ไม่มีพยากรณ์" where the source gives none.
- **A fixed footnote:** "แผ่นดินไหวพยากรณ์ล่วงหน้าไม่ได้ ข่าวที่บอกวันเวลาเกิดล่วงหน้าเป็นข่าวปลอม". Rumours of dated quakes were the Anti-Fake News Center's top item in Apr 2025 (07 §2.5 [S19]).

**Official notices** (pre-warnings)
- Each row gives the agency, the title as issued, the notice number, the area ("ครอบคลุม {จังหวัด}"), the issue time, "ถึง {date}" and [อ่านประกาศเต็ม] (a deep https page; HH rule 91).
- With no notice: "ตอนนี้ไม่มีประกาศเตือนของกรมอุตุฯ สำหรับ{จังหวัด}". If the feed failed: "ตรวจประกาศไม่ได้ตั้งแต่ {11:00} น. ดูที่ tmd.go.th".
- A standing line: "เตือนภัยจริงมาทาง SMS/Cell Broadcast ของ ปภ. และ LINE ALERT (@linealert)".

**Saved places**
- Up to 3 plus the current place, the same number as Yahoo! [A1].
- Suggested labels: บ้าน, บ้านพ่อแม่, ที่ทำงาน, or the person's own (≤ 20 characters).
- Each row shows its worst level and hazard. A tap makes it the page's place.
- Line: "เก็บในเครื่องนี้เท่านั้น ไม่ส่งไปที่ใด" (HH rule 56). Only the code and label are kept, in `localStorage`, wrapped in try/catch.

**Footer**
- Hotlines with whom they reach (HH rule 85).
- One source line per hazard with time.
- The trust strip, OSM attribution and the version.
- About (ⓘ): who made it, that it isn't official, what is stored (only on the phone) and what is sent (the OSM search text, on a tap).

### 5d. "No data", "old" and "offline"

| Case | Tile | Verdict | Sheet |
|---|---|---|---|
| The source answered, newest row within its cadence | Normal | Counts | Shows the time |
| Older than the cadence, within 3 × (e.g. PM2.5 hourly: 1–3 h) | The level's colour, desaturated, ⏱ "ข้อมูลเก่า {2} ชม." | Counts, adding "(ข้อมูลเก่า)" | "ค่านี้วัดเมื่อ {12:00} น." |
| Older than 3 × the cadence, or no answer | Hatched grey, dashes, **"ไม่มีข้อมูล"** + "ดูที่ {Air4Thai}" | **Not counted as level 1.** "ยังไม่รู้: {ฝุ่น}". The level-1 verdict adds "จากข้อมูลที่มี" | Why ("แหล่งข้อมูลไม่ตอบตั้งแต่ {10:00} น."), and the agency's own link |
| The source has nothing for this place (no station within the distance rule, no forecast) | Grey, **"ไม่มีสถานีใกล้พอ"** / "ไม่มีพยากรณ์" | As above | The nearest station and its distance, "ไกลเกินจะบอกค่าที่นี่" |
| The page is offline | A banner: "ออฟไลน์ · แสดงข้อมูลล่าสุดที่บันทึกไว้ {14:20} น." Every tile is greyed and shows its own time | The verdict is kept, prefixed "เมื่อ {14:20} น." | — |

The cadences are proposals, to be confirmed per source when the feeds are built:
- PM2.5 hourly;
- FloodWatch gauges every 10 min (3 h old → old);
- TMD warnings as issued (feed failure > 6 h → "ตรวจไม่ได้");
- heat and rain forecasts daily (> 36 h → old);
- quake feed (> 1 h without a successful read → "ตรวจไม่ได้").

### 5e. Weight and type

- **Small first paint.** HTML, CSS and JS should be well under 100 KB gzip, with data files by country or province fetched after it.
  - The 92 KB subdistrict list loads only when someone searches or taps 📍.
  - The sister app's whole start screen, with its tools, is 296 KB (AGENTS.md).
- **Fonts.** Use the system Thai font first (no download). Test a looped Thai face (Sarabun or Noto Sans Thai Looped, subset) for body text with older readers before adopting it [07 §8.4].
- **Text in rem, base 18 px,** with the "ก+" toggle remembered in `localStorage`.
- **Tone.** One calm, neutral voice for statuses, without ค่ะ or ครับ (FW 6c-1). The sister app's warmer voice suits tips, not statuses.

## 7. Gaps

- No app in §1 was opened on a phone. First screens come from listings and pages, and the NHK, HII ThaiWater, LINE ALERT and BMA rows are only partly confirmed.
- Whether `navigator.geolocation` works in LINE's in-app browser on iOS and Android isn't documented by LINE [G6]. Test it on phones.
- TMD's CAP feed was not read here, so its severity and urgency values, and the level 3/4 rule for official warnings, are unconfirmed.
- How often the nearest subdistrict centre is the wrong subdistrict is not measured.
- The heat-index level mapping (§5a) and the alarm rate it implies need the owner and a health reviewer.
- The short Thai place names (โคราช, แปดริ้ว …) for search need a native reader's list.
- The WMO slogan's exact wording [R8], the Sutton & Kuligowski panel's exact items [R7], and the full texts of [R1], [R4] and [R6, the 2018 paper] were read only as abstracts, summaries or secondary reports.
