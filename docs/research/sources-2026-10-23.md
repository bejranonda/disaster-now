# Data sources for disaster-now: the gaps, scouted live (23 Oct 2026)

This fills the gaps left by the earlier research. It does not repeat it. Read these first:

- `Hazard-huntz/docs/08-open-data.md`
- `Hazard-huntz/docs/09-server.md`
- `Hazard-huntz/docs/research/open-data-2026-10-21.md` (113 calls, 20–21 Oct 2026)
- `flood2026-research/docs/SOURCES.md` (FloodWatch's tested and refuted endpoints)

None of the endpoints that FloodWatch's SOURCES §3 refutes was called.

**How it was done.**

- **When:** 110 calls on 23 Oct 2026, 19:52–20:11 UTC (02:52–03:11 on 24 Oct, Bangkok).
- **From where:** a cloud session's proxy outside Thailand.
- **How:** `curl -gsS -L --compressed --max-time 40`, with the User-Agent `disaster-now-research/0.1 (+https://github.com/bejranonda/disaster-now)` and the header `Origin: https://disaster-now.example`, so the CORS reply could be seen.
- **Plus a few pages read:** with a search tool and a page fetcher, for terms and licences. These are marked "read" rather than "live".
- **The record:** every call is in the appendix. The raw headers and bodies stayed in the session's scratch space and were not committed.

**Limits kept.**

- **Calls per host:** about 6, with these exceptions:
  - `www.tmd.go.th` took 10, to find its storm-track data behind the page.
  - `portal.nccs.nasa.gov` took 6, plus directory listings.
- **No 429** was met.
- **Open-Meteo:** no calls to the hosts that were over the limit (`api.`, `previous-runs-api.`, `historical-forecast-api.`). The ensemble, air-quality and marine hosts answered.
- **Keys:**
  - No key was used.
  - The example `api_key` that GISTDA's own catalogue prints inside its WMS links was **not** used.
  - Neither were the tokens and map keys visible in TMD's and ASMC's page code.

**Marks.**

- ✅ a live call answered as described
- ⚠️ answered with a catch
- 🔑 needs a key or an account
- ⛔ refused or unreachable from this host. That is not proof that a phone in Thailand is refused, so the kind of failure is recorded.
- **[unverified]** seen only in documentation, a search snippet, or inferred

**CORS column.**

- `*`: any page may read the source.
- "reflects": the server echoes our origin, so the browser can read it.
- "fixed X": it only allows X, so no.
- "—": no header, so a browser can't read it.

**Bytes** are what curl counted on the wire, which is compressed when the server gzips.

## 2. The gaps, with the evidence

### Gap 1: tropical cyclones

**What answered.**

**1. JMA RSMC Tokyo** is the best fit for a phone: small, keyless and CORS `*`.

- **The list, and what it holds:**
  - `targetTc.json` (209 B, 19:54:39) listed `TC2636` (typhoon 2630, `TY`) and `TC2637` (2631, `TS`).
  - `TC2637/forecast.json` (758 B) holds `issue.UTC` 2026-10-23T19:05:00Z and, per lead time, `validtime.{JST,UTC}`, `center` [lat, lon] and `probabilityCircle.radius` (m).
    - Analysis 10.4, 166.7. +24 h: 77,784 m. +48 h: 157,420 m. +72 h: 259,280 m. +96 h: 370,400 m. +120 h: 463,000 m.
    - It also has `galeWarningArea` / `stormWarningArea`, and the past track `track.preTyphoon` / `track.typhoon`.
  - `specifications.json` (919 B) adds the name "In-fa", `maximumWind.sustained` 18 m/s (35 kt), gust 25 m/s, `pressure` "1002", and gale radii by direction.
- **Limits:**
  - JMA covers the western North Pacific and the South China Sea only. **The Bay of Bengal and the Andaman Sea are not JMA's** (RSMC New Delhi's site failed TLS here: a self-signed chain).
  - The `bosai` files are the JMA site's own data, not a documented API. JMA's terms say content "may be modified, moved or deleted without prior notice". Read them defensively.

**2. JTWC** covers both oceans, CORS `*`.

- **The RSS:** `jtwc.rss` (6.8 KB) links per-storm products: `wp2926web.txt` (warning text), `.kmz`, `.tcw`, `prog.txt` and `fix.txt`.
- **The warning text:**
  - `wp2926web.txt` (5.9 KB): "TYPHOON 29W (CHAMPI) WARNING NR 037 … 231200Z --- NEAR 25.0N 122.7E … MAX SUSTAINED WINDS - 100 KT, GUSTS 125 KT".
  - Wind radii for 34, 50 and 64 kt by quadrant, and 12-hourly forecasts.
  - Its winds are 1-minute means; JMA's and TMD's are 10-minute, so don't mix them.
- **The Indian Ocean:** `abioweb.txt` (2.0 KB, 18:00Z) is the one live source here for the Bay of Bengal. It said: "INVEST 98B … NEAR 16.2N 89.1E … POTENTIAL FOR THE DEVELOPMENT OF A SIGNIFICANT TROPICAL CYCLONE WITHIN THE NEXT 24 HOURS IS UPGRADED TO MEDIUM."
- **Format:** fixed military text, which needs a careful parser. The `.kmz` was not tested.

**3. GDACS**, CORS `*`, but large and slow.

- **The default filter:** `SEARCH?eventlist=TC` without `alertlevel` returned only Orange and Red (3 events). With `alertlevel=Green;Orange;Red` it returned 10, among them CHAMPI-26 (Red, `iscurrent` true).
- **The geometry** (`getgeometry`, 484 KB, 21.7 s) has:
  - 41 `LineString` track segments with `forecast` true or false and a category label (`TS`, `HU`)
  - 42 `Point_Polygon_Point_n` position circles
  - `Poly_Green`, `Poly_Orange` and `Poly_Red` wind buffers (labels "60 km/h", "90 km/h", "120 km/h") for each forecast time to 27/10 00:00
  - one `Poly_Cones` "Uncertainty Cones"
- **The details** (`geteventdata`, 29 KB, 32 s) give `severitydata` ("maximum wind speed of 296 km/h"). Its `cyclonesurge` was null.
- **`MAP?eventtypes=TC`** answers 400 "Eventtype is required."

**4. TMD.**

- **The page and its data:** the storm page `www.tmd.go.th/storms` loads `/CustomTheme3/js/storm/map-storm.js`, which calls `fetch('/api/Weather/StormTrack?stormId=' + id)`. `stormId=0` is "พายุที่กำลังเกิดขึ้น", the current storms.
- **The reply** (6.3 KB, no CORS): `stormTrackingData[]`, with `name` "จำปี" and `nameEN` "CHAMPI". Its `path[]` rows carry:
  - `time`: UTC with no offset. 2026-10-23T18:00:00 goes with `dateTimeTH` "24/10/2569 01:00", a Buddhist-era date in Thai time.
  - `latitude`, `longitude`, `windSpeed` (knots; the page's label is "นอต"; 0 on forecast rows), `typeEN` and `isPredictData`.
  - The forecast reaches 2026-10-26T18:00 "Low Pressure".
- **Status:** it's the page's own call, not a documented API. It is the Thai official track, so worth a collector.

**How to compute "distance from the point to the storm and its forecast track".** Used in `tc_distance.py`, in the session's scratch space:

1. **Distance to the centre now:** the great-circle (haversine) distance to the latest analysed position.
2. **Distance to the track:** the shortest distance to each segment between consecutive positions, in one of two ways. Take the minimum.
   - The cross-track distance, `asin(sin(d13)·sin(θ13−θ12))`, when the foot of the perpendicular falls inside the segment.
   - Otherwise, the distance to the nearer end.
3. **Uncertainty:** subtract JMA's `probabilityCircle.radius` at each lead time. The answer is the distance to the edge of the 70 % circle: "inside the cone" when it is ≤ 0.
4. **Wind:** test the point against GDACS' 60/90/120 km/h polygons for each time. This tells whether gale-force wind is forecast to reach the place, and when.
5. **Stating it:** "พายุอยู่ห่าง ~2,600 กม. ทางฝั่งตะวันออกเฉียงเหนือ เส้นทางคาดการณ์ไม่เข้าใกล้กว่า 2,000 กม. ใน 72 ชม.", in ranges, never an arrival minute.

### Gap 2: flood seen by satellite, flood today

**GISTDA, still keyed.**

- **Without a key:** `api-gateway.gistda.or.th/api/2.0/resources/features/flood/1day` answered 407 `{"status":407,"detail":"Authentication Required"}`, with CORS `*`.
- **The gateway** root redirects to `/v2/a/signin`, a page that needs scripts to run.
- **Getting a key:** GISTDA's catalogue entry says "สามารถสมัครเพื่อใช้งาน API key ได้ที่ https://api-gateway.gistda.or.th", meaning register there. The registration steps themselves could not be read [unverified].
- **The owner may already have one:** FloodWatch reads GISTDA flood with its own key in the `API-Key` header (its SOURCES §2k, "owner_status GFM ✅" and a GISTDA collector). Whether that key may be reused for this app is the owner's call. GISTDA echoes the key inside `links`, so strip `links` before publishing anything.
- **The other GISTDA sites:**
  - `disaster.gistda.or.th` is a Next.js page with no data in its HTML. Its API would be in its bundles, with the site's own key, and is not used.
  - `flood.gistda.or.th` failed TLS here (`SSL_ERROR_SYSCALL`). ⛔, a TLS failure, cause unknown.

**Copernicus GFM: keyless, and usable from a browser.**

- **STAC search:** `stac.eodc.eu/api/v1/collections/GFM/items?bbox=97.3,5.6,105.7,20.5&datetime=2026-10-20T00:00:00Z/2026-10-23T23:59:59Z&limit=50` answered 200, CORS reflecting our origin, `numberMatched` 132.
  - Newest: `ENSEMBLE_FLOOD_20261023T111413_VV_AS020M_E054N018T3`, datetime 2026-10-23T11:14:13Z, `created` 16:39 UTC. That is about 5.5 h of latency.
- **The assets** include `ensemble_flood_extent`, `ensemble_likelihood`, `exclusion_mask`, `reference_water_mask`, `advisory_flags`, and a `tilejson` built on a **WGS84 copy** (`data.eodc.eu/collections/GFM_WGS_NRT/observed_flood_extent/…tif`).
- **A point:** `titiler.services.eodc.eu/cog/point/104.1,21.99?url=<that COG>` → `{"coordinates":[104.1,21.99],"values":[0.0]}`. Use 0 = no flood seen, 1 = flood, 255 = no data (FloodWatch's reading of the layer).
  - `cog/info` gave the COG's real bounds (the imaged strip). These are smaller than the STAC item's box: a point outside them gives 500 "Point is outside dataset bounds".
  - A range read of the COG answered 206 with CORS `*`, so a phone could also read the pixel itself (geotiff.js), with no third service.
- **Caveats** (FloodWatch, D-069):
  - The item's box is not the imaged strip.
  - Radar is blind in towns.
  - About 6 passes over a region in 30 days.
  - Say "water seen", never "not flooded".

**NASA/UMD flood product, now VIIRS.**

- **The layers:** GIBS lists `VIIRS_Combined_Flood_{1,2,3}-Day` (NOAA-20 + NOAA-21, times to 2026-10-23) beside `MODIS_Combined_Flood_*` (to 2026-10-23).
- **A map:** a 256×456 WMS `GetMap` over 5–21 N, 97–106 E for 2026-10-22 answered 200, CORS `*`.
- **The colours,** from the colour map `colormaps/v1.3/MODIS_Flood.xml`:
  - 0 No Water (transparent)
  - 1 Surface Water `50,210,245`
  - 2 Recurring Flood `255,255,0`
  - 3 Flood `250,30,36`
  - 255 Insufficient Data `175,175,175`
- **That day's pixels:** 63,393 Insufficient Data, 29,458 Surface Water (sea included), 624 Recurring Flood, 129 Flood.
- **Use:** a phone can ask for a tiny box around its point and read the pixel on a canvas (CORS `*` allows it). In the monsoon it will often say "cloud".

**Sentinel Asia:** `sentinel-asia.org/EO/EmergencyObservation.html` (HTML, no CORS) lists Thai activations. The newest is "Thailand on 26 September, 2026". Use it as a link, not data.

**ONWR's official area warning:**

- **Via the tile service:** the per-area JSON API (`api.waterrisk.onwr.go.th`) failed again here (proxy 502; the system resolver has no address for it, the DNS failure docs/09 recorded). But **its tile service on Google Cloud Run (Bangkok region) answered**.
  - `/layers` lists `flood-warn`, `flood-forecast-d0…d3`, `flood-dep-tambon-d0…d3`, `flood-area-poly`, `rain-radar` ("Radar: Thai Meteorological Department (TMD)", 15 min), `rain-noaa`, `rain-30min` and `heat-index-now/3h/6h/9h`.
  - `flood-warn` was updated 2026-10-23T16:28:42Z and `flood-area-poly` 2026-10-22T07:38:43Z.
- **Reading it:** FloodWatch decoded the warning cells (about 1.1 km hexagons, `class_risk` 1 = เฝ้าระวัง, 2 = แจ้งเตือน in the API's own terms). See its SOURCES §2p, ⚠️ legend mismatch. A phone can fetch the one `.pbf` tile containing its point (CORS reflects).

### Gap 3: drought

**GISTDA (keyed).** The catalogue (`opendata.gistda.or.th/api/3/action/package_search?q=drought`, 4 datasets, "License not specified") gives two services:

## 4. Recommended additions to the app, in order

1. **Storm watch, mostly from the phone.**
   - **Each visit:** read JMA's `targetTc.json`. For each storm, fetch `forecast.json` and `specifications.json`, all under 1 KB with CORS `*`.
   - **Every few hours:** read JTWC's `abioweb.txt` and `abpwweb.txt`, also CORS `*`. They are the only source here for the Bay of Bengal and for invest areas before naming.
   - **The rule:** compute the distance to the centre, the track and the 70 % circle (gap 1). Show a line only when the forecast track or circle comes within, say, 1,000 km.
   - **The collector** adds TMD's `StormTrack` JSON (the Thai name and the official track). When a storm is within range, it adds GDACS' wind polygons, too slow for the phone (484 KB, 20–30 s).
2. **TMD's keyless XML into the collector.** These need no key and so unblock phase 1 before the TMD key arrives:
   - the newest `storm-tracking` item (the current announcement text, beside CAP's province codes)
   - `region-daily-forecast` for the province's max/min
   - hourly `weather-report` for a small set of stations: present weather, as a thunder proxy
3. **"Water seen near you", from the phone.**
   - **ONWR:** the `flood-warn` and `flood-forecast-d0…d3` tile containing the point, the official area warning for today and +1…+3 days (CORS reflects; check `tilejson.data_updated`).
   - **GFM:** a STAC search for the newest Sentinel-1 item over the point, then a pixel via titiler or a direct COG read ("seen flooded on 23 Oct 11:14 UTC").
   - **GIBS VIIRS:** a 2-Day pixel, saying "cloud" honestly.
   - **Later:** GISTDA, once keyed.
   - **The wording:** never "not flooded".
4. **Fire danger from the phone:** GWIS `nasa_geos5.query` `GetFeatureInfo` for today and the next days (CORS `*`), after confirming the value order with JRC. Show it only in the dry season, beside FIRMS hotspots within 50–300 km.
