# D11: should the page compute earthquake chances itself? (24 Oct 2026)

Research note for ภัยรอบบ้าน · Thai Risk, written for the owner's decision D11 ([OWNER_ACTIONS](../OWNER_ACTIONS.md)).
The owner asked: "Should we compute or recompute from the forecasting related to the other parameters ourself? You
can deep research, validate and suggest." I read it two ways and answer both:

- **(a)** Should the page compute its own aftershock / short-term earthquake chance (Reasenberg–Jones with generic
  parameters, as USGS does by default; or ETAS), or **recompute** an agency's forecast for the visitor's place (turn a
  USGS aftershock forecast into "the chance of feeling shaking here" with the page's intensity equation)?
- **(b)** Do **other parameters** (tides, rain and season, foreshocks, b-value changes, radon, animals, ionospheric
  signals, AI on catalogues) carry any short-term skill that a page could use?

It extends the earlier study of today, [predict-earthquake-2026-10-24.md](predict-earthquake-2026-10-24.md) (called
"the earlier study" below), and does not repeat it. Every number comes from a script in `research/` (prefix
`eq_d11_`) whose log is in `research/logs/` (commands in §8). Nothing was changed on the page. Anything not confirmed
from a primary source is marked **[unverified]**.

## 0. Answer in brief

**(a) No to both, for now: words and links only, as D11 proposes, with three wording changes.**

- **The earlier "no skill" verdict was half right.** The generic chance USGS uses by default really does
  over-forecast this region, and that result is robust (it held on a fairer real-time set of 567 quakes and in
  cross-validation). But "a regional refit only tied the base rate" was **underpowered**: if the refit had been the
  truth, that test would have noticed only 27 % of the time. Cross-validated over all 567 sequences, two methods beat
  the plain base rate modestly for "another M ≥ 5 near the first quake within a week":
  - the history of quakes the same size: Brier skill +0.08 from 1 h, +0.12 from 1 day;
  - USGS's generic model updated with the first day's aftershocks: +0.21. This is closer to how USGS uses it, since
    USGS combines generic and sequence-specific values and now runs ETAS.
- **Those chances are for the zone around the first quake, not for the visitor's place.** For a place in Thailand
  (15 cities, 2015–2026):
  - **A weekly chance computed from the catalogue gains nothing over each place's own long-term rate.** ETAS:
    Brier skill −0.07 for "M ≥ 5 within 300 km", −0.02 for "may be felt here". A logistic regression and boosted
    trees do no better (0.00 to −0.02).
  - **After a quake of M ≥ 6 that the card lists**, there were only 9 such quakes in 12 years (50 city-windows).
    By the page's own intensity estimate none of them was followed, after the first hour, by shaking "อาจรู้สึกได้"
    (estimated intensity 2.5 or more) at a city. Every model said about 10–15 %.
  - **People did report light shaking in 4 of the 50 windows** (USGS "Did You Feel It?", 1–6 responses each). That
    is mainly because the page's equation reads aftershocks below M5.5 about 1–2 intensity units low (the felt-word
    domain, GUIDELINES 3e). No such window had a report of IV or more.
  - **Even if ETAS were exactly right, the record could not show it.** About 36 years of quakes would give 60 % power
    for "may be felt here". A chance of shaking at IV or more could not be checked even with 360 years (20 %).
- **Recomputing USGS's forecast for a place** was possible only once in this region (the 2025 M7.7). Its first
  version, recomputed, would have told Mae Hong Son 43 %, Chiang Mai and Bangkok 28 % "may be felt within a week",
  and 3–5 % for IV. The page's equation says nothing reached 2.5 after the first hour. People in Chiang Mai reported
  CDI 3.2 for one M5.1 aftershock on day 2; the equation gives that quake 1.4 there.
  - The recomputed number stacks three uncertain steps: USGS's count, where the aftershocks fall, and an intensity
    equation used outside its domain. The three methods tried here disagree by a factor of 2–4.
  - It would also be the page's own number dressed as USGS's.
- **In 2015–2026 every week in which a city's estimated shaking reached IV was a mainshock week that no model saw
  coming** (the 2025 M7.7; the 2019 Laos M6.2 at Nan). ETAS gave those weeks 0.06–0.18 %, below each city's own rate.
  The only case where a chance after a quake "came true" for a Thai city was a foreshock: the 2019 Laos M5.7 was
  followed 2 h 47 min later by the M6.2. ETAS had put "IV at Nan within a week" at 6.7 %, 23 times Nan's usual rate.
  That is real information, but a percentage the page's chance rule (tens, never 0) cannot even display.

**(b) No.**

- **Tides:** no significant tidal phase in 1,285 independent quakes. The largest modulation seen would change a
  chance by at most about 1.1 times.
- **Season:** slightly *fewer* quakes in the monsoon months for the whole box; nothing in the north.
- **Foreshocks:** 5 % (2015–2026) to 8 % (1973–2014) of M ≥ 5 quakes were followed within a week and 50 km by a
  larger one. That is 15–27 times the background, but it is the same clustering that ETAS models, not a separate
  predictor.
- **b-value:** cannot be measured in real time from this catalogue. In the first day, sequences have a median of 0
  events above completeness.
- **Machine learning:** see (a); it does no better than each place's long-term rate.
- **Radon, animals, ionosphere and AI** have no demonstrated operational skill in the literature or for any agency
  (§2).

**What to ship (§5):**

1. The aftershock line in words: reworded, shown only after a listed M ≥ 6 for 7 days, never lifting the tile.
2. The USGS forecast link, now saying that its chances are for the area around the fault, not for this place.
3. A corrected reason in the D11 rule text.

**Don't ship:**

- any number or "N times usual" from our own or a recomputed model;
- any level change from a forecast;
- anything from "other parameters".

## 1. What the earlier verdict rested on, and how robust it is (`eq_d11_recheck.py`)

**What was tested** (earlier study §4):

- **Quakes and windows.** 391 mainshocks M ≥ 5.5 in the box 0–30 °N, 88–110 °E: 322 for training (1973–2014) and
  69 for the test (2015–2026). The forecast was issued 1 h after each one.
- **Event.** At least one ComCat quake M ≥ 5 (or ≥ 4) inside USGS's circle (10^(0.69 M − 3.22) km around the
  epicentre) within 7 or 30 days.
- **Models.** The Page et al. (2016) generic Reasenberg–Jones model (G); a regional refit (R); the training base rate
  (B0); the base rate by mainshock magnitude (B1).
- **Scores.** Brier score, Brier skill vs B0, log score, paired bootstrap.
- **Result.** G was worse than B0 on all four targets (e.g. M ≥ 5 in 7 d: BSS −0.91, interval −0.108 … −0.022).
  R and B1 had intervals that included zero.

**Four weaknesses, and what each changes.**

1. **Power.** The test held 5–12 events for the M ≥ 5 targets. I simulated 2,000 outcome sets with R's (or B1's)
   probabilities as the truth and applied the earlier criterion (bootstrap 95 % lower bound above zero):

   | target | true model | its expected skill (BSS) | detected in |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | M ≥ 5 in 7 d | R | +0.15 | **27 %** |
   | M ≥ 5 in 7 d | B1 | +0.16 | **24 %** |
   | M ≥ 5 in 30 d | R / B1 | +0.15 / +0.17 | 29 % / 28 % |
   | M ≥ 4 in 7 d | R / B1 | +0.19 / +0.09 | 56 % / 23 % |
   | M ≥ 4 in 30 d | R / B1 | +0.25 / +0.12 | 76 % / 36 % |

   So "R and B1 tied the base rate" is weak evidence of no skill; the test would usually miss real skill of this
   size. The verdict on G (worse than the base rate) does not depend on power: its interval was below zero.

2. **Hindsight in choosing the quakes.** "Mainshocks" were the Gardner–Knopoff independent events, which are known
   only afterwards. An M5.6 that was later followed by a bigger quake was labelled a "foreshock" and dropped, even
   though a page would have shown a line after it. **The real-time set** used here takes every M ≥ 5.5 in its circle,
   except one with a quake at least as large within the previous 7 days. That gives **567 sequences** (480 train,
   87 test); by the earlier labels, 364 independent, 63 "foreshocks" and 140 "aftershocks".
   - 19 of the 567 (3.4 %) were followed by a *larger* quake inside the circle within 7 days: M5.5–6.0 16/389,
     M6.0–6.5 2/129, M6.5–7.0 1/35, M ≥ 7 0/14.
   - On the 2015–2026 test (n = 87) the verdict does not change:
     - G is still worse than B0 (M ≥ 5 in 7 d, BSS −0.59, interval −0.092 … −0.023);
     - R (−0.09) and B1 (−0.09) have intervals that include zero.

3. **More test sequences: leave-one-era-out cross-validation.** The eras are 1973–89, 1990–99, 2000–09, 2010–14 and
   2015–26; R and B1 were refitted without the era being scored. This is **not prospective**, since later eras train
   earlier ones, but it scores all 567 sequences:

   | issued | target | n / observed | B1 by magnitude | R refit | G generic | GU generic + first-day update |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 h | M ≥ 5 in 7 d | 567 / 79 | **+0.081** (+0.002 … +0.019) | +0.063 (−0.001 … +0.016) | **−0.132** (−0.030 … −0.001) | – |
   | 1 h | M ≥ 5 in 30 d | 567 / 97 | **+0.124** | **+0.077** (+0.001 … +0.022) | −0.115 | – |
   | 1 h | M ≥ 4 in 7 d | 567 / 185 | **+0.070** | +0.038 | **−0.449** | – |
   | 1 d | M ≥ 5 in 7 d | 567 / 52 | **+0.119** | RU +0.110 (incl. 0) | −0.042 | **+0.208** (+0.003 … +0.032) |
   | 1 d | M ≥ 5 in 30 d | 567 / 74 | **+0.173** | RU +0.011 | −0.031 | **+0.198** (+0.007 … +0.038) |
   | 1 d | M ≥ 4 in 7 d | 567 / 140 | **+0.100** | RU +0.103 | **−0.415** | **−0.139** (below 0) |

   - Each cell is the Brier skill vs B0, with the bootstrap 95 % interval of Brier(B0) − Brier(model) where it
     matters; **bold** means the interval excludes zero.
   - The refit's Omori exponent ran into its lower bound (p = 0.3) in 4 of 5 folds, so the Omori form describes these
     counts poorly, as the earlier study saw.

4. **No sequence update.** USGS updates its forecast with the aftershocks it records. The earlier study tested only
   the generic forecast at 1 h and 24 h. GU is the forecast issued 1 day after: the a-value prior is updated by the
   count of events above the time-dependent completeness Mc(t) in the first day. That update fixes much of G's
   over-forecast for M ≥ 5 (above). For M ≥ 4 it still over-forecasts: mean 0.49 against 0.25 observed.
   - On the 87 test sequences alone, GU and B1 again have intervals that include zero.
   - 67 of the 87 test sequences had **no** counted event in their first day, so the update usually only lowers the
     chance.

**Corrected verdict.**

- The generic chance over-forecasts this region (robust).
- A chance from the history of same-size quakes, or from the generic model updated on the first day, carries modest
  skill for the zone around the first quake (cross-validated). It was not shown prospectively, because 2015–2026 is
  too short.
- None of this is about the visitor's place; §3.3 tests that.

## 2. What the literature and the agencies say (short)

Sources are numbered in §9 and were all read on 2026-10-24. Types: **PR** peer-reviewed, **OP** official or
operational, **NEWS**, **OTHER**. A literature scout read them; the author re-read [1], [2], [3], [4] and [5] (marked
✓). A source the scout could read only as an abstract (paywall or bot check, not bypassed) is marked "abstract".

**Agencies' operational practice** (new beyond the earlier study §1.4):

- **USGS.** The public forecast "began in August 2018" (Michael et al. 2020 [6], PR), which settles the earlier
  study's **[unverified]** start year. Since then the message has been "a tiered template that provides basic
  information first", changed after the media "merged the probability of one or more events … with the likely range
  of the number of events" [6]. On space: "At this time, we are not calculating spatial forecasts or providing maps
  to show areas with the highest likelihood …" (OAF overview ✓ [1], OP). Its international ETAS product has "a map
  of the probability of experiencing strong shaking", but "USGS does not make quantitative international forecasts
  public without an express request by the country in question" (Mizrahi et al. 2024 review [7], PR). **USGS itself
  does not publish a chance of shaking at a place** for this region.
- **New Zealand (GeoNet).** It **does** publish shaking chances for places, from its own models (STEP, ETAS, EEPAS
  hybrids [7]): "The maps show the probability of MM6 and MM7 shaking within the aftershock region"; "Over the next
  year the probability of MM7 shaking is largest in the area of Cape Campbell at around 6%"; "the probability of MM7
  shaking in the Wellington area is around 3% … in the next year" (Kaikōura forecast, issued 21 Feb 2025 ✓ [2], OP).
  The forecast pages pair chances with words (e.g. 29 % "unlikely", 4–6 % "very unlikely", "less than 1%" "extremely
  unlikely", on the IPCC/WMO pattern [7]). The scout could not read the exact band edges **[unverified]**. Mizrahi et
  al. note "hesitation to forecast earthquakes of magnitude M < 5.0" and no testing "of the public forecasts
  themselves" [7].
- **Italy (OEF-Italy).** Weekly forecasts of ML ≥ 4 and ≥ 5.5 and of MMI ≥ VI–VIII on a 0.1° grid, "not open to the
  public and is accessed only by authorized personnel" [7]. Spassiani et al. 2023 [8] (PR, abstract) report "good
  performance skill … except" when catalogue incompleteness in the 2016–17 sequence led to under-forecasting.
- **Japan (JMA).** In words, with a proportion rather than a chance: "過去の事例では、大地震発生後に同程度の地震が発生した割合は
  １～２割あることから、揺れの強かった地域では、地震発生から１週間程度、最大震度７程度の地震に注意してください" (JMA Toyama, on the 2024
  Noto quake ✓ [3], OP). That is: in past cases a quake of similar size followed in 1–2 of 10, so where shaking was
  strong, watch for about a week. Since 2016 its numbers have been multiples, not percentages (earlier study [15]).
- **Testing (CSEP).** Clustering models beat time-independent ones by large factors during sequences: "probability
  gains up to 1000 during major aftershock sequences" (Rhoades et al. 2018 [9], PR). The ICEF puts "nominal gains of
  100-1000" for short-term clustering models but notes that for L'Aquila "the 1-day probability remains much below
  1%" [10]. UCERF3-ETAS at Ridgecrest "can be informative" but "mildly overpredict" (Savran et al. 2020 [11], PR).
  The region-level ETAS here (§3.2) agrees: a large gain per event, over-forecast after events.
- **Thailand, Myanmar, Laos.** No forecast by TMD, DMR or Myanmar's DMH was found.
  - In the news, TMD's count of the M7.7's aftershocks was given beside "อาฟเตอร์ช็อกที่เกิดขึ้นมีความรุนแรงต่ำ
    หลายพื้นที่ของประเทศไทยแทบไม่รู้สึกถึงแรงสั่นไหว" (Thai PBS, 29 Mar 2025 [12], NEWS). Whether that sentence is TMD's
    is **[unverified]**.
  - TMD said of one aftershock "มีขนาดเล็กไม่ส่งผลกระทบต่อประเทศไทย", and the article counted 207 aftershocks in
    28–31 Mar (Thai PBS, 31 Mar 2025 [13], NEWS).
  - A 2026 study of the sequence with TMD's catalogue (complete to about ML 3.0) found "α =2.023, indicating a reduced
    capacity to trigger secondary aftershocks, while p =1.17, reflecting rapid decay" and "89% of M L ≥5.5
    aftershocks occurred within 16 days" (Zhao et al. 2026 [14], PR, abstract).
  - A Chulalongkorn thesis reports that the gap between mainshock and largest aftershock in Thai zones is smaller
    than at plate boundaries ("0.4-2.5 …", Traitangwong 2018 [15], OTHER). Sainyabuli 2019's largest aftershock
    (Mw 5.5) came in 2021 (Sethanant et al. 2025 [16], PR).

**Other parameters:**

| parameter | verdict | key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Earth tides | detectable for some faults, not operational | USGS ✓ [4]: "The increased probability is a factor of ~3 during high tides … raising this tiny probability by a factor of 3 during high tides still results in a very tiny probability"; Cochran et al. 2004 [17] (PR, abstract): rate varies "by a factor of 3 with the tidal stress" (shallow thrusts); for M ≥ 8 by date or lunar cycle, Hough 2018 [18] (PR): "No." Here: no significant phase (§3.5) |
| Rain, season | modest modulation of small quakes in some places, never forecast-tested | Nepal "37 ± 8% fewer earthquakes in the summer than in the winter" (Bollinger et al. 2007 [19], PR); California "modestly modulated" (Johnson et al. 2017 [20], PR); USGS [21]: "approximately an equal distribution of earthquakes in cold weather, hot weather, rainy weather". Here: 0.88 times the rate in May–Oct (§3.5), the same sign as Nepal |
| Foreshocks | about 5 % of quakes are followed by a larger one; they cannot be told apart beforehand | USGS ✓ [5]: "the likelihood that an earthquake will be followed by a larger earthquake nearby and within a week is about 5%"; ICEF [10]: foreshocks "have not displayed special rupture characteristics that allow them to be discriminated a priori"; worldwide rates exceed California's "by a factor of ∼2", lower before strike-slip quakes (Reasenberg 1999 [22], PR). Here: 5.0 % (2015–2026), 8.2 % (1973–2014) (§3.5) |
| b-value (traffic light) | contested, not operational, needs a dense low-completeness network | Gulia & Wiemer 2019 [23] (PR): 95 % accuracy on 58 sequences, retrospective; "most often results in ambiguous warning levels" and needs "dense instrumentation, consistent (versioned) low completeness catalogs" (Dascher-Cousineau et al. 2020 [24], PR); biases "have the same direction as the proposed signal" (van der Elst 2021 [25], PR). Here: not measurable (§3.5) |
| Radon | no demonstrated skill | ICEF [10]: "they do not offer support to the hypothesis that radon anomalies are diagnostic precursors"; "only about 10% of the total observed anomalies" were pre-seismic |
| Animals | no demonstrated skill | "only 14 time series were published", and part of the reports "are in fact related to foreshocks" (Woith et al. 2018 [26], PR); ICEF [10]: "no credible scientific evidence"; USGS [27]: reliable behaviour "still eludes us" |
| Ionosphere, EM, thermal | no demonstrated skill | Heki 2011's TEC rise [28] is "explained by the depletion of TEC due to tsunami" (Kamogawa & Kakinami 2013 [29], PR) and "likely … artifacts" (Eisenbeis & Occhipinti 2021 [30], PR); no significant change "when considering all 1279 earthquakes" (Thomas et al. 2017 [31], PR); ICEF [10]: "too weak to provide any probability gain" |
| AI / ML | no operational gain over ETAS | meta-analysis of 77 papers: "far simpler (and traditional) models seem to offer similar predictive powers, if not better ones" (Mignan & Broccardo 2020 [32], PR); Google's encoder ">4% improvement", about "0.1 information bits per earthquake" (Zlydenko et al. 2023 [33], PR); "none of the five NPPs tested outperform ETAS" (Stockman et al. 2024 [34], preprint); the UT Austin "70%" trial used EM/acoustic sensors, with a mean location error of "381 km" (Saad et al. 2023 [35], PR). Here: ties or loses (§3.3) |

PNSN's four categories (earlier study §0) still frame it:

- **prediction:** none;
- **early warning:** a phone push, not this page;
- **long-term probabilities:** the 2564 zones;
- **short-term forecasts:** real and operational where an agency has its own catalogue and models, published as the
  agency's words and numbers. No one publishes them for Thailand.

## 3. Experiments

All runs use ComCat (USGS), box 0–30 °N, 88–110 °E, M ≥ 4, 1973-02-10 to 2026-10-23: 19,718 events with the earlier
study's Gardner–Knopoff flags (`research/data/eq_comcat/`, downloaded by `eq_fetch_catalog.py`). ComCat is complete
here from about M 4.5–5.0. The page's intensity equation is `public/js/ipe.js` (Allen, Wald & Worden 2012), plus 1.06
for greater Bangkok within 1,600 km for quakes in the term's domain (`quake.inSiteDomain`). The 15 cities are the
earlier study's. My scripts made **no** HTTP calls; they reuse the earlier study's files.

### 3.1 Recheck of the earlier test

See §1 (`eq_d11_recheck.py`; log `eq_d11_recheck-2026-10-24.log`, output `research/out/eq_d11_recheck.json`,
per-sequence table `research/out/eq_d11_realtime_sequences.csv.gz`).

### 3.2 A regional ETAS model (`eq_d11_etas.py`)

**Design** (fixed before fitting):

- **Model.** Space-time ETAS (Ogata 1998) with an isotropic power-law kernel.
- **Magnitudes.** M0 = 5.0. Gutenberg–Richter b from the Aki–Utsu estimate on M ≥ 5, 1973–2014: **b = 1.118**.
- **Background.** Gaussian-smoothed (100 km) independent M ≥ 5 epicentres, with a 5 % floor (the earlier study's
  choice, §6).
- **Fit.** 1,996 target events in 1–29 °N, 89–109 °E, 1980–2014, with parents since 1973. Maximum likelihood.
- **Test.** Parameters frozen, 2015-01-01 to 2026-10-16.

**Fitted parameters:**

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| μ | 0.040 per day |
| A | 1.95 |
| α | 1.12 |
| c | 0.0094 d |
| p | 1.019 |
| D | 8.1 km |
| γ | 1.26 |
| q | 1.81 |

- **Background share:** 26 % of the fit period's events.
- **Direct M ≥ 5 aftershocks from 1 h to 7 d:** 0.51 after an M6, 1.58 after an M7, 3.46 after an M7.7.
- **Branching ratio.** It is 3.45 to infinite time, which is meaningless with p ≈ 1. Within 1 / 10 / 30 years it is
  0.62 / 0.74 / 0.80.
- **α is low** (0.49 per magnitude unit in log10; Reasenberg–Jones scaling is about 1). Small quakes count as
  productive and great ones as unproductive. This is typical of a catalogue whose early aftershocks after big quakes
  are missing and whose magnitudes mix mb and Mw.

**Region-level test (2015–2026, 424 M ≥ 5 events):**

| measure | ETAS | Poisson | observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| expected events | 517 | 672 (training rate, including 2004–05) | 424 |
| information gain per event | **1.79 nats** (probability gain 6.0) | – | – |
| daily-updated forecast, gain over 4,306 days | **+66 nats** | – | – |
| "≥ 1 M ≥ 5 in the region tomorrow": Brier skill | **+0.045** | – | – |

- In the reliability table, days ETAS put at 0.2–0.4 came true only 14 % of the time (mean forecast 0.25), so ETAS
  over-forecasts after events.
- **Clustering is real and forecastable here, as everywhere.** The question for the page is whether that reaches a
  place.

### 3.3 Chances at Thai places (`eq_d11_point.py`, `eq_d11_point_extra.py`)

**Design** (fixed before any point score; a 7-day window throughout):

- **Targets:**
  - **A:** ≥ 1 ComCat quake M ≥ 5 within 300 km (the card's radius);
  - **B:** ≥ 1 quake M ≥ 4.5 with page-estimated intensity ≥ 2.5 at the city ("อาจรู้สึกได้");
  - **C:** ≥ 1 quake with page-estimated intensity ≥ 4.0 (MMI IV).
- **Schemes:**
  - **W (weekly):** every 7 days from 2015-01-01, at 15 cities, 9,225 city-weeks;
  - **T6 (triggered):** issued 1 h after each ComCat M ≥ 6.0 that the card would list at the city (within 300 km or
    estimated ≥ 2.5), non-overlapping per city;
  - **T55:** the same with M ≥ 5.5 (secondary).
- **Models:**
  - **C:** the city's own climatology (training weeks; A from 1980, B and C from 1995);
  - **E:** ETAS (§3.2), first generation;
  - **BT / B1T:** pooled training share after triggers (all / by trigger magnitude);
  - **GP:** "the agency's generic forecast recomputed for the place": Page et al. generic Reasenberg–Jones for the
    trigger's regime, aftershocks uniform over USGS's circle, magnitudes b = 1 down to 4.5, plus the climatology;
  - **Z:** "say nothing" (p = 0, so its Brier equals the observed share).
- **Bootstrap** by issue week (W) or by trigger quake (T).

**Weekly, unconditional (test, 9,225 city-weeks):**

| target | observed | climatology Brier | ETAS mean p | ETAS BSS (95 % of Brier(C) − Brier(E)) | gain, nats per week |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, M ≥ 5 within 300 km | 30 (0.33 %) | 0.00322 | 0.0054 | **−0.066** (−0.00073 … −0.00001) | −0.0014 |
| B, may be felt (≥ 2.5) | 60 (0.65 %) | 0.00652 | 0.0098 | −0.016 (−0.00038 … +0.00005) | +0.0016 |
| C, IV | 7 (0.08 %) | 0.00077 | 0.0008 | +0.005 (−0.00001 … +0.00001) | +0.0016 |

- ETAS over-forecasts A about twice at Thai places (weeks put at 1–2 %: 0.7 % observed; at 5–10 %: 3.1 %).
- The seven city-weeks with IV were the 2025 M7.7 week (Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, Mae Hong Son, Mae Sot, Nan)
  and the 2019 Laos week (Nan). **ETAS had given them 0.06–0.18 %**, at or below the cities' own rate (0.29–0.58 %):
  the shaking that mattered came from mainshocks that nothing foresaw.
- **Machine learning** (fixed design, no tuning; logistic regression and gradient-boosted trees on catalogue
  features; trained 1980–2014): A, BSS −0.008 and −0.020; B, −0.000 and −0.009. Neither beats the climatology.

**After a listed M ≥ 6 (T6).**

| set | windows (quakes) | observed A / B / C | ETAS mean p A / B / C | BSS vs C, ETAS A / B / C | GP mean p B / C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train 1980–2014 (in-sample for ETAS and BT) | 292 (51) | 12 / 37 / 6 | 0.049 / 0.078 / 0.009 | +0.48 / +0.18 / +0.22 (intervals mostly include 0) | 0.20 / 0.047 |
| **test 2015–2026** | **50 (9)** | **1 / 0 / 0** | 0.083 / 0.106 / 0.010 | −1.35 / below 0 / below 0 | 0.15 / 0.023 |

- In the test set every model over-forecast "may be felt" (B): 0 observed against means of 0.11 (ETAS), 0.13 (the
  training share BT) and 0.15 (GP).
- **Post-hoc, against what people reported (DYFI, CDI within 20 km of the city):** 4 of the 50 windows had a report
  ≥ 2.5, each from 1–6 responses:
  - Bangkok after the M7.7;
  - Chiang Mai after the M7.7, an M5.1 at CDI 3.2 from 6 responses;
  - Chiang Mai after the 2019 Laos M6.2;
  - Phuket after the 2016 M6.5.

  Against those, ETAS scores −0.05, GP +0.07 and BT +0.02, all intervals including zero. No window had a report ≥ 4.
  The page's equation under-reads these aftershocks (M4.4–5.1 here): for the M5.1 of 29 Mar 2025 it gives 1.4 at
  Chiang Mai and 1.3 at Bangkok. This is the known bias for M < 5.5 (+1.26; MODELS), outside the felt words' domain.
- **T55 (M ≥ 5.5 triggers, test, 63 windows from 14 quakes):**
  - A: 5 observed (7.9 %); ETAS +0.21, GP +0.28, both intervals including zero.
  - The events came almost all from one sequence: the Laos M5.7 of 2019-11-20 21:03 UTC, followed 2 h 47 min later
    by the M6.2. ETAS had given A 0.28–0.31 at Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, Udon Thani and Nan, and IV at Nan 0.067 against
    a climatology of 0.0029 (23 times).
  - Post-hoc DYFI: 10 of 63 windows had reports ≥ 2.5, and there ETAS (+0.25, interval +0.003 … +0.093) and GP
    (+0.20, +0.001 … +0.070) beat the climatology. This was chosen after the fact and leans on one foreshock
    sequence.
- **Power** (ETAS taken as the truth, trigger quakes resampled; about 9 M ≥ 6 triggers per 12 years at these
  cities):

  | target | ~12 years | ~36 years | ~120 years | ~360 years |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | A, M ≥ 5 within 300 km | 18 % | 60 % | 98 % | 100 % |
  | B, may be felt | 25 % | 62 % | 98 % | 100 % |

  T55 is similar (A 29 / 73 / 100 / 100 %; C 0 / 0 / 6 / 22 %). **A chance of being felt at IV at a Thai place can
  never be checked against Thai history; "may be felt" would need decades.**

### 3.4 USGS's own forecast for the 2025 M7.7, recomputed for Thai cities (`eq_d11_oaf_point.py`)

**Method** (fixed before results):

- From each of the 75 issued versions: the M ≥ 5 count fractiles, magnitudes by the version's b (truncated at
  M 8.5), depth 10 km.
- Where in the circle: **S1** uniform over USGS's circle, or **S2** the ComCat aftershocks recorded before the
  version, smeared by 20 km.
- P = 1 − E[(1 − q)^N], with q the chance that one aftershock reaches the threshold at the city.
- One sequence, overlapping versions: **a case study, not a validation**.

**Results, 1-week window:**

|---|---|---|---|
| first (issued 28 Mar 15:20 UTC, window from 9.7 h; 196-km circle; USGS P(≥ 1 M ≥ 5) = 0.97) | Mae Hong Son | 0.43 / 0.43 | 0.053 |
| first | Chiang Rai | 0.30 / 0.29 | 0.03 |
| first | Chiang Mai | 0.28 / 0.28 | 0.03 |
| first | Bangkok | 0.28 / 0.29 | 0.03 |
| first | Nan | 0.18 / 0.18 | 0.02 |
| first | Mae Sot | 0.16 / 0.16 | 0.01 |
| second (400-km circle) | Chiang Mai | 0.40 / 0.24 | – |
| second | Mae Hong Son | 0.53 / 0.40 | 0.155 / 0.052 |

- The 1-year windows (50 versions) give ≥ 2.5 at Chiang Mai 0.43 / 0.30 and IV 0.09 / 0.04.
- **Observed, by the page's equation:** no quake in any version's circle reached 2.5 at any of these cities after the

- **By DYFI:** Chiang Mai CDI 3.2 (6 responses) and Bangkok 3.3 (2 responses) for the M5.1 of 29 Mar 09:20 UTC near

- How the spatial assumption behaves:
  - The S1 / S2 difference is small for the first version.
  - For later versions it is a factor of 1.7–3: USGS's 400-km circle puts aftershocks close to Thailand that never

  - **A per-place number recomputed from USGS depends on an assumption USGS does not make.**

## 4. Why "no" for (a), in one place

1. **The page's question is about a place; the evidence is about a zone.** The modest skill found (§1) is for
   "another M ≥ 5 near the first quake". At Thai places the chances after a big quake were
   mostly under 5 % for IV and 2–50 % for "may be felt" (higher only right next to the quake, as at Chiang Rai
   in 2014). They cannot be validated on the Thai record (§3.3 power), and the methods disagree 2–4 times.
2. **It would break the page's own rules.**
   - The felt words rest on M ≥ 5.5 quakes, while most aftershocks are smaller, so a felt chance from them is outside
     the domain (GUIDELINES 3e).
   - The chance rule (tens, never 0) cannot show 2 %, and a "0" or "10 %" would be wrong.
   - JMA stopped publishing low percentages because they read as reassurance (earlier study [15]).
   - "Multiples of usual", JMA's replacement, came out 4–26 times for the same city and quake depending on the
     method (Chiang Mai after the M7.7: ETAS 3.7×, GP 13× for IV). That is not a stable number to print.
3. **Recomputing USGS's forecast** makes the page's own number look like USGS's (GUIDELINES §1 (2): the agency's word
   leads, never re-graded). And USGS published one for only two quakes in the region since 2015.
4. **What people need after a big quake is behaviour, not a percentage:** more quakes come, most early, and one can
   be strong. The agencies say this in words (PNSN, JMA, TMD in the news), and บ้านรอดไหม has the actions.

## 5. Recommendation and wording

| # | item | category (PNSN) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aftershocks in words after a listed M ≥ 6 (reworded) | forecast, behaviour only | **ship** after the owner checks it against บ้านรอดไหม |
| 2 | Link to USGS's aftershock forecast when one exists (reworded: "for the area around the fault") | forecast, the agency's | **ship as a link, no numbers** |
| 3 | The D11 rule text with a corrected reason | – | **adopt** |
| 4 | Our own chance (ETAS, Reasenberg–Jones, history), any target | forecast | **don't ship** |
| 5 | USGS's forecast recomputed for the place ("x % may be felt here") | forecast | **don't ship** |
| 6 | "N times the usual chance" | forecast | **don't ship** |
| 7 | Any level change from a forecast | – | **don't ship**: the quake tile keeps its level from quakes that happened (24 h) |
| 8 | Tides, season, foreshock or b-value signals, radon, animals, ionosphere, AI | – | **don't ship** |

**1. Aftershocks in words.**

- **When it shows:** the card lists, among quakes less than 7 days old, one of M ≥ 6.0 (within 300 km, or estimated
  "อาจรู้สึกได้" or more at the place: the card's existing filter). It shows once, under that quake's line, for 7
  days from it. The 7 days follow JMA's "about a week" and the window tested here; the line never says "a week",
  which would read as an all-clear afterwards.
- **When it says nothing:** no such quake in the card, or the quake sources failed. The card's existing error line
  stands; the aftershock line is never a substitute for data. Never for quakes below M6.0, never in the verdict, and
  it never changes the tile's level.
- TH: "หลังแผ่นดินไหวใหญ่ มักมีแผ่นดินไหวตามมา (อาฟเตอร์ช็อก) ช่วงแรกเกิดบ่อยแล้วค่อยๆ ห่างขึ้น แต่ครั้งหลังก็อาจแรงได้
  และบางครั้งแรงกว่าครั้งแรก"
- EN: "After a big quake, more quakes (aftershocks) usually follow: often at first, then less often. A later one can
  still be strong, and sometimes it is stronger than the first."
- **Sources:**
  - PNSN [earlier study 37]: "Larger earthquakes trigger more aftershocks", "The rate of aftershocks decreases with
    time", "The magnitudes of the aftershocks do not necessarily get smaller with time".
  - JMA [earlier study 14; 3]: watch for a quake as large as the first for about a week; in past cases one of
    similar size followed in 1–2 of 10.
  - TMD in the news: "แผ่นดินไหวตาม" / "อาฟเตอร์ช็อก" (§2).
  - **Data for "sometimes stronger":** 3.4 % of real-time M ≥ 5.5 triggers within 7 d in the circle; 5–8 % of M ≥ 5
    within 50 km (§1, §3.5). Too rare for "often", too common to leave out.
- **What changed from the earlier study's line:** "แต่ครั้งหลังก็อาจแรงได้" became "… และบางครั้งแรงกว่าครั้งแรก",
  because foreshocks are the one case where the chance after a quake came true at a Thai city (Laos 2019). JMA's
  advice to say "地震" rather than "余震" exists because people take aftershocks to be smaller.
- **Any action** ("what to do") stays บ้านรอดไหม's (`npm run check:advice`). The card's link "แผ่นดินไหว ทำอย่างไร"
  already gives it.

**2. USGS's forecast as a link.**

- **When it shows:** the USGS event of a listed quake carries an "oaf" product. One extra cached call per M ≥ 6 in
  the card, as the earlier study §8 #9 describes.
- TH: "USGS เผยแพร่การคาดการณ์อาฟเตอร์ช็อกของแผ่นดินไหวครั้งนี้ เป็นโอกาสสำหรับบริเวณรอบรอยเลื่อน ไม่ใช่คำทำนาย และไม่ได้บอกว่าที่นี่จะรู้สึกหรือไม่"
- EN: "USGS has published an aftershock forecast for this quake: chances for the area around the fault, not a
  prediction, and not whether it will be felt here."
- **Why the new clause:** USGS's first forecast for the M7.7 said 97 % for an M ≥ 5 within a week inside a 196-km
  circle. Recomputed for Chiang Mai, that is about 28 % for even light shaking, and nothing above the page's
  threshold came (§3.4). A visitor must not read 97 % as "here".
- No number is copied (earlier study §8 #9; JMA's reassurance problem).

**3. The rule text (GUIDELINES §7, D11).** The earlier proposal said "the aftershock chances tested on this region
had no skill over the base rate". That reason is not accurate (§1). Proposed:

> **Nothing predicts earthquakes** (when, where and how big: USGS, ICEF, TMD). **The page computes no earthquake
> chance of its own and recomputes no agency's chance for a place:** the generic aftershock chance over-forecast this
> region, and a chance for a place in Thailand cannot be checked against the record (too few quakes; the felt words
> rest on M ≥ 5.5) (docs/research/eq-d11-2026-10-24.md). It may (a) say in words how aftershocks behave, (b) link to
> an agency's published aftershock forecast without copying its numbers, worded as chances for the area around the
> fault, never as a prediction, and (c) show long-term hazard only as an official source states it (the 2564 zones;
> GEM by link), labelled long term. Early warning is a phone push, not this page: the page may only say where to
> switch it on.

**Optional, low priority (not needed):** a line in the quake guide, not the card, saying that animals, weather, the
Moon or "earthquake clouds" can't tell a quake in advance. Only with USGS's own wording (§2) and บ้านรอดไหม's
agreement; there is no evidence that visitors need it.

## 6. What would change the recommendation

**For computing our own chance (items 4–6).** All of these, not one:

1. **A regional reference.** An agency (TMD, USGS) or a peer-reviewed study publishes forecast parameters for this
   region, or a regional ETAS that is calibrated here. The one fitted here over-forecasts Thai places about twice.
2. **A prospective record.** A research job (like `research/archive_forecasts.mjs`) saves the would-be chance after
   every qualifying quake, and the record passes a pre-registered test. By §3.3 that takes on the order of 30–40
   trigger quakes for "may be felt", which is decades at Thailand's rate. It could be shortened only by pooling with
   other regions under one method (CSEP-style). For IV it is not reachable from Thai history at all.
3. **The intensity equation validated below M5.5** (now +1.26 too low there) and for offshore quakes. A felt chance
   for aftershocks rests on it.
4. **A Thai wording test** of how a small chance is read (JMA's 2016 experience). The page's chance rule would need a
   new form for chances under 10 %.
5. **A Thai local catalogue** (TMD) with a low completeness, so that sequence updates and b-values become measurable
   (median 0 counted events in the first day now).

**If the owner ever wants one number,** the most defensible form is JMA's: a historical proportion for the zone,
not a chance for the place. Here that is "a larger quake followed within a week". It came out at 3.4 % for M ≥ 5.5
triggers inside USGS's circle (1973–2026), and at 5.0 % (2015–2026) or 8.2 % (1973–2014) for M ≥ 5 within 50 km. That
spread with the definition is one more reason to keep it in words ("บางครั้ง", "sometimes").

**For recomputing an agency's chance (item 5).**

- If **USGS or TMD publishes a spatial or shaking-probability forecast** (a map of the chance of intensity ≥ x), the
  page could show the agency's own number for the place, quoted and linked as theirs, under rules 3b/3c (a forecast
  alone tops out at เตรียมตัว).
- If **TMD ever issues an aftershock forecast or advisory**, it is quoted verbatim and leads, as TMD's warnings do.

**For other parameters (item 8).** A prospective, peer-reviewed demonstration of probability gain beyond ETAS,
adopted by an agency. None exists (§2).

## 7. Caveats, and what was not done

- **One catalogue.** ComCat only, mixed mb/Mw, complete from about M 4.5–5.0. TMD's catalogue counts many more
  aftershocks (207 in four days after the M7.7, by the news) and was not available. ISC was not used.
- **"Observed" felt shaking is the page's own estimate** for B and C. DYFI was used post-hoc and is a floor (people
  report only when they felt something and know the site).
- **The ETAS model** is first-generation in its forecasts (aftershocks of aftershocks inside the window are left
  out), isotropic, with one parameter set for the whole box. The 2004–05 Sumatra–Andaman sequences dominate the fit.
  A mainland-only or fixed-α variant was not tried.
- **GP spreads aftershocks uniformly over USGS's circle.** USGS's operational ETAS is not reimplemented. Only one
  sequence has USGS forecasts to recompute.
- **Overlap.** Windows at neighbouring cities share quakes. The bootstrap resamples whole weeks or whole trigger
  quakes for that reason, but 9–14 trigger quakes remain 9–14 cases.
- **Post-hoc analyses** are labelled where they appear (DYFI outcomes).
- **Rules kept.** No HTTP calls by the scripts. Web reading for §2 was done with search tools only, with the URLs and
  read dates in §9. `nice -n 19`; venv in the session scratchpad; no container touched; `.env` not read. Files
  created only as `research/eq_d11_*.py`, their logs and outputs, and this report.

## 8. Reproduce
