## 4. Forecast validation

```text
You are the forecast-validation researcher for a new Thai app, "disaster-now" (repo /home/user/disaster-now; put your work in research/ and docs/research/ only). ## What the app will compute (test exactly this)

Per member: heat index each hour → max over rolling windows 0–24 h, 24–48 h, 48–72 h; rain sum per window; max gust per window. The app shows the ensemble median and the 10–90 % range, and probabilities = fraction of members over a threshold (P(rain_24h ≥ 35 mm), P(HI ≥ 42 °C), P(gust ≥ 40 km/h)). The deterministic forecast API (api.open-meteo.com, best_match) is a fallback.
- Heat index: US NWS method in °F then converted: simple Steadman formula HI = 0.5·(T + 61 + (T−68)·1.2 + RH·0.094); if (HI+T)/2 ≥ 80 °F use the Rothfusz regression with the two NWS adjustments (RH < 13 % and 80–112 °F: subtract; RH > 85 % and 80–87 °F: add). Write it once in research/heat_index.py with a docstring citing the NWS page, plus 6 test vectors (T °C, RH %, HI °C) taken from that method, so the app's JS can be checked against the same vectors.
- Levels (Thai): heat index เฝ้าระวัง 27.0–32.9, เตือนภัย 33.0–41.9, อันตราย 42.0–51.9, อันตรายมาก ≥ 52 °C (TMD/DOH); PM2.5 24-h mean bands 15 / 25 / 37.5 / 75 µg/m³ (PCD 2023); rain 24 h: 10.1 / 35.1 / 90.1 mm (TMD — to be confirmed by a parallel researcher).
- PM2.5 outlook: CAMS via Open-Meteo air-quality API, corrected with the latest observation: ratio = (observed 24-h mean) / (CAMS 24-h mean over the same past hours), clipped to [0.5, 3], applied to the next 24–72 h. Observations at the point come from GISTDA getPm25byLocation — for history use Air4Thai.
- Earthquakes: an intensity-prediction equation (IPE) to say whether a recent quake was probably felt at the point (no forecasting of quakes — say so).

## Experiments (priority order; do as many as you can, report all honestly)
E3 PM2.5: CAMS history from Open-Meteo air-quality vs Air4Thai hourly observations (build a CA bundle with Let's Encrypt's YR1 and root-yr-by-x1; never disable verification; find the right history parameters). Compare for next-24 h daily mean and hourly: (a) raw CAMS, (b) persistence, (c) the ratio-corrected CAMS, (d) a linear blend fitted on training data, (e) optionally a small gradient-boosting model — only if it beats (d) on the disjoint test. Dry season: train Dec 2025–Feb 2026, test Mar–Apr 2026, confirm on other stations and on Oct 2026 if available. Metrics: MAE, bias, band agreement, POD/FAR/CSI for ≥ 37.6 and ≥ 75.1. Note CAMS history is stitched short-lead forecasts (an upper bound).
E4 Earthquake felt intensity: implement one published IPE, coefficients copied exactly from the paper or USGS ShakeMap/OpenQuake code (cite). Validate against USGS "Did You Feel It?" for events felt in Thailand (2025-03-28 M7.7 Myanmar, 2014-05-05 Mae Lao, Laos 2019, Myanmar 2011 Tarlay…). Report MAE in MMI by distance band, and propose the app's wording rule; note Bangkok's soft-soil amplification if the data shows it.
E1 Heat index and E2 heavy rain at 24 h and 48 h lead: forecast archives — try (a) Open-Meteo previous-runs (over limit at 19:45 UTC; retry once after 00:10 UTC), (b) whether the ensemble API accepts past dates, (c) ECMWF open-data archive on AWS (bucket ecmwf-forecasts, CC BY 4.0; byte-range GRIB2 via .index files; eccodes/cfgrib), (d) NOMADS GFS (last ~10 days). Observations: NOAA ISD hourly on S3 for ~10–14 Thai synoptic stations. Metrics: heat — MAE, bias, band agreement, POD/FAR for HI ≥ 33 and ≥ 42, baselines persistence and station-month climatology; rain — POD/FAR/CSI/frequency bias for ≥ 35 and ≥ 90 mm, Brier score and reliability for probabilities, baseline climatology. Candidate correction: linear a + b·forecast, chosen on an earlier period, scored on a later one, confirmed on held-out stations.
Flood: don't build a flood model. Report FloodWatch's own validated skill from its docs/MODELS.md.

## Output
1. docs/research/forecast-validation-2026-10-23.md: per experiment — question, data, method, results vs baselines, verdict (use as is / with correction / don't show), caveats; then "What the app should say about forecast confidence".
```
